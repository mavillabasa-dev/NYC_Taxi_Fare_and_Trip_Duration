Market Friction & Customer Expectation

Traditional taximeters have the disadvantage of computing fares after the ride concludes. Today, passengers expect upfront price and travel time certainty before committing to a trip.

We want a solution where we predict both fare amount and duration simultaneously in milliseconds using strictly pre-ride metadata (zones, timestamp, passenger count) without any in-transit telemetry.

Anti-Leakage Principle: Standard random splitting allows target encodings and pricing patterns from future rides to contaminate past training. Temporal splitting guarantees that the model is evaluated strictly on chronological forward simulation.

3. Scope & Solutions · Feature Transformations

From 6 raw inputs to 29 features.

Temporal Features
(10 Features)

• Cyclical Hour & Day (sin/cos): Transforms 24h & 7d cycles into continuous coordinates to avoid midnight/weekend boundary jumps.
• Rush-Hour Flag: Captures morning and evening commuter traffic congestion peaks.
• Holiday & Weekend Flags: Adjusts for Memorial Day weekend and off-peak demand shifts.

Spatial Features
(10 Features)

• Zone Centroid Coordinates: Joins TLC shapefile latitude and longitude for pickup & dropoff zones.
• Haversine & Manhattan Distance: Computes direct geodesic and street-grid driving distances.
• Airport Zone Indicators: Flags JFK, LaGuardia, and Newark airport flat-rate and highway trips.

Categorical Features
(3 Features)

• Historical Target Encodings: Maps high-cardinality zone IDs to historical mean fares & durations.
• Bayesian Smoothing ($m=10$): Shrinks low-volume routes toward the global prior to avoid variance.
• Fitted Strictly on Train: Calculated only on training data to prevent lookahead data leakage.

Allowed Raw Inputs
(6 Features)

• PULocationID: Pickup TLC zone (1–265).
• DOLocationID: Dropoff TLC zone (1–265).
• Pickup Datetime: Trip departure timestamp.
• passenger_count: Rider count (1–9).
• RatecodeID: TLC tariff rate code tier.
• trip_distance: Estimated GPS route miles.

3. Scope & Solutions · Feature Contract

Guaranteed zero post-trip leakage.

Allowed Inputs at Prediction Time (6 Columns)

PULocationID DOLocationID tpep_pickup_datetime passenger_count RatecodeID trip_distance

Documented Assumption: trip_distance is the only post-trip feature allowed. In our prototype UI, users are provided with an estimated route distance directly; in a full production system, an external routing engine (e.g., OSRM / GPS navigation) would compute the exact driving distance upfront prior to departure.

Banned Post-Trip Columns (8 Columns)

tpep_dropoff_datetime fare_amount total_amount tip_amount tolls_amount mta_tax payment_type congestion_surcharge

Leakage Prevention Rationale: These fields are only generated after trip completion and directly encode our prediction targets (e.g., dropoff timestamp yields exact duration; total/tolls/taxes yield fare). Including them causes severe target leakage and lookahead bias, rendering models useless for live pre-ride quotes.

4. Metrics & Results · Multi-Family Exploration

Three distinct model families tested.

Family 1: Baselines

Trivial & Trees

• DummyRegressor(strategy="mean"): Predicts global target means ($15.15 fare, 16.5 min duration) to establish the mathematical floor.
• DecisionTreeRegressor(max_depth=12, min_samples_leaf=50): Constrains tree depth and minimum leaf size to prevent memorizing outlier rides.
• LinearRegression(): Simple linear baseline behind median imputer to evaluate linear feature interactions.
• Why these models: Establishes sanity benchmarks and non-linear baseline thresholds that all production candidates must beat.

Family 2: Gradient Boosting

LightGBM & XGBoost

• LGBMRegressor(num_leaves=63, n_estimators=400, learning_rate=0.05): Leaf-wise growth with L1 loss to directly minimize MAE at high speed.
• XGBRegressor(max_depth=6, subsample=0.8, reg_lambda=5.0): Depth-wise trees with L2 shrinkage regularization to control model variance.
• TimeSeriesSplit(n_splits=3): Splits data chronologically forward in time rather than randomly, eliminating future-to-past data leakage.
• Why these models: Industry standard for tabular data, delivering top accuracy, rapid training, and sub-millisecond serving latency.

Family 3: Neural Networks

Multi-Layer Perceptron

• MLPRegressor(hidden_layer_sizes=(64, 32), activation="relu"): Two fully connected layers preceded by StandardScaler.
• Adam(early_stopping=True, learning_rate_init=0.001): Halts training upon validation loss plateau to prevent overfitting.
• Why this model: Benchmarks continuous representation learning and deep non-linear interactions against tree ensembles.

6. Conclusions & Next Steps · Assumptions & Tradeoffs

Documented assumptions & tradeoffs.

1. Temporal Scope

Single-Month Window (May 2022)

• Dataset Boundary: Models are trained exclusively on 3.5M records from May 2022 (late spring weather and school-year traffic).
• Unobserved Seasonality: The pipeline cannot capture annual seasonality shifts, such as summer vacation drops or winter holiday shopping peaks.
• Macroeconomic Drift: Fares reflect May 2022 fuel surcharges and base rate rules prior to subsequent TLC rate increases.

2. Dynamic Weather

Unobserved Meteorology

• Atmospheric Friction: Sudden torrential rainstorms, heavy snowfall, and icy roadways significantly reduce citywide travel speeds.
• Telemetry Absence: The current pipeline captures seasonal calendar cycles and US holidays, but does not ingest live NOAA meteorological feeds.
• Operational Impact: Extreme weather increases duration MAE during storms, whereas regulated fares remain comparatively predictable.

3. Demand & Road Incidents

Regulated Rates vs. Gridlock

• Regulated Tariff Structure: Unlike ride-hail platforms with dynamic surge multipliers, NYC yellow cabs operate on fixed, TLC-regulated rate formulas.
• Unscheduled Road Disruptions: Unplanned bridge maintenance, tunnel closures, or emergency vehicle blocks introduce localized travel delays.
• Operational Impact: Models predict expected travel times under normal conditions rather than transient incident shocks.

6. Conclusions & Next Steps · Engineering Roadmap

Potential production enhancements.

1. Routing Engine Integration

OSRM Microservice

• Dynamic Road Topology: Embed an open-source OSRM container into Docker Compose to query turn-by-turn road networks.
• Upfront Feature Sourcing: Replace centroid Haversine proxies with live road driving distance and routing geometry before pickup.
• Low-Latency Architecture: In-memory OpenStreetMap graph traversal preserves sub-10ms response budgets.

2. MLOps Monitoring

Drift & Telemetry

• Data & Concept Drift: Integrate Evidently AI and Prometheus to detect shifts in feature distributions, speed trends, and ridership patterns.
• Telemetry Metrics: Track serving latency percentiles (P95, P99), prediction distributions, and unexpected error spikes.
• Proactive Alerting: Trigger automated notifications when production inference inputs deviate from training baseline statistics.

3. Continuous Retraining

Automated Pipelines

• Scheduled Ingestion: Deploy Apache Airflow or GitHub Actions to ingest monthly TLC parquet releases as new datasets publish.
• Automated Champion-Challenger: Evaluate newly trained candidate models against the active production champion on holdout data.
• Versioned Artifacts: Track experiment runs in MLflow and package serialized model bundles with cryptographic validation.

6. Conclusions & Next Steps · Summary

Key project takeaways.

1. Rigorous Engineering

Zero Data Leakage

• Strict Temporal Boundary: Chronological train/test splitting (May 1–22 vs May 23–31) eliminated lookahead bias and simulated authentic production deployment.
• Inference Feature Contract: Dropping 13 post-trip telemetry columns guaranteed models predict strictly with data available at pickup.
• Robust Validation: Out-of-fold target encoding and chronological cross-validation ensured strong generalization without temporal contamination.

2. Production Performance

High Accuracy & Speed

• Benchmark Winner: LightGBM achieved $1.42 Fare MAE (R²=0.952) and 3.76 min Duration MAE (R²=0.797), outperforming baselines and MLPs.
• Sub-2ms In-Process Speed: Pure model scoring averaged 1.40 ms locally, enabling high-throughput quotation on minimal CPU hardware.
• L1 Objective Optimization: Direct training on Mean Absolute Error minimized training loss while maintaining high resilience against taxi outlier rides.

3. Modular Microservices

Isolated Containers

• Self-Contained Artifact: A single 4.2 MB serialized bundle packages preprocessing and dual models with zero training overhead.
• Decoupled Microservices: Independent FastAPI backend and Streamlit dashboard communicate via REST over Docker networks.
• Pre-Ride Certainty: Successfully replaces drop-off meter surprises with transparent, instant pre-ride quotes before the passenger enters the cab.

Assumptions & Tradeoffs

Presenter: Néstor Mamani6. Conclusions1:00

English Script

We document three operational assumptions and engineering tradeoffs. First, temporal scope: our models are trained exclusively on 3.5 million records from May 2022, capturing late spring patterns but unobserved to annual seasonality shifts like summer vacation drops or winter blizzards. Second, dynamic weather: sudden thunderstorms cause speed reductions that our calendar indicators cannot observe without live meteorological telemetry. Third, real-time demand and traffic shocks: while yellow cab fares follow regulated formulas, unscheduled road closures introduce travel duration variance.

Spanish Script

Documentamos tres supuestos operativos y compensaciones de ingeniería. Primero, alcance temporal: los modelos se entrenaron exclusivamente con 3.5 millones de registros de mayo de 2022, capturando la primavera tardía pero sin observar estacionalidad anual como vacaciones de verano o tormentas de nieve en invierno. Segundo, clima dinámico: tormentas repentinas reducen la velocidad y no son observadas por variables de calendario sin telemetría meteorológica en vivo. Tercero, tráfico en tiempo real: aunque las tarifas de taxi son reguladas, incidentes viales imprevistos introducen varianza en la duración.
